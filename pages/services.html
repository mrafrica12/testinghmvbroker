Individual & Family Health Insurance

The right time to shop for health coverage depends on your needs, but the sooner you understand premiums, deductibles, copays, and out-of-pocket maximums, the better prepared you'll be. We help you compare HMO, PPO, and high-deductible plans before open enrollment, coordinate coverage if you're transitioning from an employer plan, and check whether you qualify for subsidies or tax credits.

Common Questions

What's the difference between an HMO and a PPO?
An HMO requires you to use an in-network primary care doctor and get referrals for specialists, usually at a lower cost. A PPO costs more but lets you see any provider, in or out of network, without a referral.
When can I enroll in individual or family health insurance?
Open enrollment runs once a year, but qualifying life events — losing employer coverage, marriage, a new baby, moving — open a special enrollment window. We can tell you which window applies to your situation.

Life Insurance

Life insurance is one of the most selfless gifts you can give the people you'd leave behind. It's also one of the more overlooked retirement income tools available — permanent life insurance has grown in popularity as a retirement funding option because of its tax advantages in a world of fluctuating markets. We compare term, whole, and blended policies sized to your actual needs, not a one-size quote.

Common Questions

How much life insurance do I actually need?
A common starting point is 10–12x your annual income, adjusted for debts, a mortgage, and future costs like college. We run the specific numbers for your household rather than applying a flat rule.
Can life insurance be part of my retirement plan?
Yes — permanent life insurance builds cash value you can access tax-advantaged in retirement, which is why some clients use it alongside (not instead of) traditional retirement accounts.

Auto & Homeowners Insurance

Your home and car are two of your most valuable — and most personal — investments. About 1 in 20 insured homes files a claim each year, most often for wind, hail, water damage, or fire, and the U.S. sees over 6 million police-reported car crashes annually. As life changes — a new driver, home upgrades, a move — your coverage needs to change with it, which is why we review policies regularly rather than setting and forgetting them.

Common Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?
No — standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage. Flood coverage requires a separate policy, typically through the National Flood Insurance Program, which we can help you evaluate if you're in a flood-prone area.
How much auto insurance coverage do I need in Georgia?
Georgia requires minimum liability limits, but minimums often aren't enough to protect your assets in a serious accident. We help you size liability, collision, and comprehensive coverage to your actual risk.

Retirement Planning

Whether retirement is 10 years out or already here, the plan changes with the timeline. Ten years out, you're maxing out retirement vehicles while you still have time to grow. Five years out, it's about shifting to a safer, shorter-term strategy that still allows growth. One year out, it's about defining what your retirement lifestyle actually looks like and testing your post-retirement budget before you need it.

Common Questions

When should I start planning for retirement?
As early as possible — but the strategy that matters most is the one for whatever stage you're in now, whether that's 10 years out, 5 years out, or already retired.
How is retirement planning different from a 401(k)?
A 401(k) is one savings vehicle. Retirement planning is the broader strategy — how you'll turn savings, Social Security, and other income sources into dependable income once paychecks stop.

Income Planning

Saving for retirement and generating income in retirement are different problems. Income planning is about converting what you've saved into a reliable paycheck-replacement — sequencing withdrawals, Social Security timing, and guaranteed-income tools so you don't run out of money before you run out of retirement.

Common Questions

What is income planning, exactly?
It's the strategy for converting savings into a steady stream of retirement income — deciding what to withdraw, when, and from which accounts, so your money lasts as long as you need it to.
How is income planning different from investing?
Investing focuses on growing assets. Income planning focuses on distributing them — timing withdrawals and income sources for tax efficiency and longevity, not just growth.

Long-Term Care Insurance

At least 70% of people over age 65 will need long-term care services at some point. Without a plan, that cost typically comes straight out of retirement savings — which is why it's worth deciding how you'll pay for care, and sharing that plan with whoever may need to make decisions on your behalf, before it becomes urgent.

Common Questions

What does long-term care insurance actually cover?
It covers costs standard health insurance and Medicare largely don't — nursing home care, assisted living, and in-home care for help with daily activities like bathing or dressing.
When should I buy long-term care coverage?
Earlier is cheaper — premiums rise with age and health changes can affect eligibility, so most people buy in their 50s or early 60s rather than waiting until care is imminent.

Disability Income Protection

Most people insure their life but not their income — even though you're statistically more likely to become disabled for 90+ days before age 65 than you are to die. Employer group disability plans typically cap out around 60% of salary or $5,000/month, and Social Security disability approves roughly 30% of applicants. A personal policy fills that gap.

Common Questions

What does disability income insurance cover?
It replaces a portion of your income — typically 50–65% — if illness or injury keeps you from working. Policies vary on how "disabled" is defined, so we walk through that language before you buy.
Is my employer's disability coverage enough?
Often not on its own. Group coverage is a good baseline, but the payout caps and the fact that benefits are usually taxable mean many people layer an individual policy on top.

Commercial Insurance

Over 30% of small businesses experience property damage, liability claims, or a data breach each year, and a single incident can jeopardize everything you've built. From general liability and commercial property to cyber liability, workers' compensation, and business interruption coverage, we build a protection plan that grows with your business rather than a generic policy you outgrow in a year.

Common Questions

What does general liability insurance cover?
It covers third-party bodily injury, property damage, and related legal costs if your business is found responsible — the baseline policy most commercial leases and contracts require.
Do I need cyber liability insurance for a small business?
If you store customer data, take card payments, or rely on email and cloud tools, yes — a breach or ransomware incident can cost far more than the policy, and general liability typically doesn't cover it.

Group Benefits

A survey by Glassdoor found 79% of employees prefer better benefits over a higher salary — making a group benefits package one of the most effective ways to attract and retain talent. We help business owners build and manage group health and ancillary benefit plans that compete for talent without blowing up the budget.

Common Questions

What's included in a typical group benefits package?
Most commonly group health insurance, plus optional ancillary lines like dental, vision, life, and disability — bundled to give employees meaningful coverage at group rates.
How many employees do I need to offer group health insurance?
Small-group plans are typically available starting at 1–2 employees in Georgia, though rates and requirements vary by carrier. We can tell you what your business currently qualifies for.
